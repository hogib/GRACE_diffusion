## Summary of the sub-field

**Task taxonomy.** ML/DL work on GRACE/GRACE-FO TWSA falls into six task families:
1. **Gap filling.** This covers the 11-month GRACE/GRACE-FO gap (Jul 2017 to May 2018) and the roughly 20 missing months inside the GRACE record. Examples are Sun et al. 2019, Mo et al. 2022, Uz et al. 2022, Lai et al. 2022, Zhang et al. 2022 and Wang et al. 2024 (GAIN).
2. **Reconstruction beyond the mission record.** Examples are GRACE-REC (Humphrey & Gudmundsson 2019), Li et al. 2020 and 2021, Sun Z. et al. 2020, GTWS-MLrec (Yin et al. 2023), GRAiCE (Palazzoli et al. 2025), DeepRec (Gentner et al. 2025), Mandal et al. 2025, RecGAN (Wang et al. 2024) and an MTGNN graph network (Arzoumanidis et al. 2026).
3. **Spatial (and temporal) downscaling.** Early work fitted pixel-wise RF, BRT, ANN or XGBoost models at coarse scale and applied them at fine scale (Miro & Famiglietti 2018, Seyoum et al. 2019, Rahaman et al. 2019, Chen et al. 2019, Zhang et al. 2021, Yin et al. 2022, Agarwal et al. 2023). Later work uses image-to-image CNNs and U-Nets (He et al. 2021, Gou & Soja 2024, Uz et al. 2026, Li et al. 2026, Goracci et al. 2026), GANs (Gu et al. 2024, Wang et al. 2025, Hassanibesheli et al. 2024, D-SHIFT 2026) and temporal downscaling to daily fields (Uz et al. 2024, D-SHIFT).
4. **Forecasting and nowcasting.** These works predict TWSA or groundwater months ahead. Examples are Ahmed et al. 2019 (ANN), Mo et al. 2025 (BCNN over the roughly 3-month product latency), Li F. et al. 2025 (ML combined with CLM data assimilation), Steidl et al. 2026 (hierarchical GNN plus LSTM, 6 months ahead), Kiani Shahvandi 2026 (PINN, decadal) and many regional LSTM/BiLSTM studies (Lu et al. 2024).
5. **Learned correction of GRACE errors.** Examples are ML selection of which coefficients to decorrelate (Piretzidis et al. 2018), CNN leakage and signal restoration from SH coefficients (Zhang Q. et al. 2023), a CNN with spatial attention that maps Level-2 data to TWSA while removing stripes (Chen W. et al. 2025, JSTARS) and pix2pix or NN signal separation (Heller-Kaikov et al. 2023, 2025). Sun et al. 2019 corrects the model rather than GRACE: a CNN learns the NOAH–GRACE mismatch.
6. **Generative models and uncertainty quantification (UQ).** UQ approaches include SVGD Bayesian CNNs (Mo 2022, 2025), deep ensembles (Gou & Soja 2024), MC dropout plus a variational U-Net (Uz 2026), statistical ensembles (GRACE-REC, 100 members) and probabilistic PINNs (Kiani Shahvandi 2026). Gou et al. 2026 (Surveys in Geophysics) surveys UQ for deep-learning-derived ECVs. The only generative-diffusion work is Deep-GRASP (Kiani Shahvandi 2026, Geodesy and Geodynamics), and its full text could not be obtained (see below).

**Inputs and predictors.** Nearly every study uses meteorological forcing: precipitation, 2 m temperature and evapotranspiration from ERA5/ERA5-Land, GLDAS, CRU, GPCC or MSWEP. Many add storage from land-surface or hydrological models: GLDAS-Noah TWSA (Sun 2019), ERA5-Land TWSA and cumulative water-storage change (Mo 2022), WGHM (Gou & Soja 2024, Uz 2026, RecGAN), PCR-GLOBWB 2 (Li 2026), EALCO (He 2021) or VIC. Downscaling studies add static or high-resolution predictors such as a DEM, land cover, NDVI/LAI, solar-induced fluorescence (GRAiCE), SMAP soil moisture, LST and impervious surface (GTWS-MLrec). Climate indices (ONI, DMI) are selected in Mandal 2025. Several studies feed past TWSA (Mo 2025) or SLR low-degree fields (Uz 2024 SciData).

**Architectures.** The progression runs from MLP/ANN, RF, XGBoost and SVR, through LSTM, BiLSTM and GRU per pixel or basin, to 2D CNNs taken from segmentation (VGG16, U-Net, SegNet in Sun 2019) and super-resolution (SRCNN, VDSR, RCAN in He 2021). From there it moves to residual encoder-decoders with self-supervised dual losses (Gou & Soja 2024), variational U-Nets (Uz 2026), ConvLSTMs, attention hybrids (CNN-BiLSTM-attention), GNNs (MTGNN, HiGNN), ESRGAN-type GANs (D-SHIFT) and cGANs (RecGAN, Hassanibesheli). The one claimed DDPM is Deep-GRASP, which couples a U-Net, MLPs and a ConvLSTM.

**Evaluation and typical numbers.** The usual metrics are Pearson R/CC, NSE, RMSE/NRMSE and KGE. Studies evaluate against GRACE itself on held-out months and add indirect checks: water-balance closure (P − ET − R), wells, streamflow, sea-level budget, glacier mass and GNSS loading. Representative numbers:
- Sun 2019 (India): CNN-corrected NOAH reaches country-average R = 0.94 and NSE = 0.87.
- Mo 2025: 1–3-month-ahead global predictions reach median R = 0.92–0.95, NSE = 0.81–0.89 and RMSE = 1.79–2.26 cm.
- Gou & Soja 2024: area-weighted basin RMSE of 21.9 mm against GRACE over 288 basins, versus 49.2 mm for WGHM.
- Li 2026 (China, 0.1°): median CC 0.95 and RMSE 1.38 cm at 3°.
- Steidl 2026: one-month-lead RMSE of 1.83 cm versus 3.70 cm for climatology. Skill is lost by 6 months.
- Arzoumanidis 2026: grid-cell R = 0.69 and basin R = 0.94.
- Zhang 2026 (1 km GWSA): re-aggregated R² = 0.972, but only R² = 0.538 against held-out wells.

Two things recur. Scores at GRACE scale are near-perfect because the loss enforces them, while scores against independent fine-scale data are much lower. And skill drops sharply in arid, irrigated and glaciated regions.

**Weaknesses.**
1. **Deterministic, conditional-mean outputs.** Almost all models regress the mean, so outputs are overly smooth and under-represent extremes and fine-scale variance. D-SHIFT injects noise and uses an adversarial loss partly to counter this, and its authors report the result as extra high-frequency noise and spurious daily excursions.
2. **Thin UQ.** Where uncertainty is given, it is per-pixel spread from SVGD particles, deep ensembles, MC dropout or seed ensembles. None of these gives spatially coherent sample fields, and none is checked for calibration (no CRPS, spread–skill or rank histograms).
3. **Weak physical consistency.** Mass conservation is a soft loss term (Gou & Soja, Uz, Li, Goracci). Constrained designs exist (Sarkar 2026 basin mass balance plus atmospheric-supply trend bounds, Wu 2026 mass-conserving XGBoost, Vishwakarma 2021 catchment-scale PLSR conservation). No deep-learning model represents the actual GRACE observation operator (SH truncation, filter or mascon averaging) or its correlated error covariance. The closest is the non-DL Bayesian GMRF of Pakrashi et al. 2026: its likelihood ties CSR mascon blocks and wells to a latent fine-scale field and it returns posterior SD maps, but it uses block averages rather than the true operator.
4. **Inherited priors and missing human signals.** Fine-scale structure comes from WGHM, PCR-GLOBWB or ERA5, and climate-driven reconstructions such as GRACE-REC are explicitly detrended. These models therefore miss or mis-place irrigation, groundwater depletion, reservoirs and glaciers (Sun 2024 commentary; Xiong 2026; Suryawanshi 2026).
5. **Small samples and leaky validation.** There are about 230 monthly fields. Some studies split months at random (GTWS-MLrec; Uz et al. 2024 SciData, 135/57 random months) or use hyper-parameter-tuning years as the "validation" set (D-SHIFT). Counter-examples with sequential splits are Arzoumanidis 2026, Lu 2024 and Steidl 2026.
6. **No fine-scale truth.** Validation below about 300 km is indirect.

**Open gaps that diffusion and score-based models could address.**
- Ensembles of spatially coherent, calibrated realisations: the posterior over fine-scale fields consistent with GRACE.
- Explicit likelihood or guidance through the true GRACE measurement operator. Diffusion posterior sampling could replace the soft averaging loss.
- Better behaviour on extremes and spectra than MSE regressors.
- A single generative prior that serves gap filling (inpainting in time), reconstruction (conditional generation from forcing), downscaling (super-resolution) and short-range forecasting.
- A probabilistic benchmark (CRPS, spread–skill) across U-Net, GAN, tree and diffusion baselines on closed-loop data (cf. Goracci 2026 NGGM/MAGIC E2E simulations).

Deep-GRASP claims a DDPM for downscaling, assimilation and prediction, but it is unreplicated and only the abstract could be read. Its sampling, conditioning and calibration are unknown, so the gap remains essentially open.

**Diffusion-for-GRACE search result.** I searched OpenAlex, arXiv (abstract queries "GRACE AND storage", "GRACE AND generative", "gravimetry AND neural"), web search (several phrasings including score-based, DDPM and flow matching), the GFZ GRACE publication list (4,131 entries, grepped for ML terms) and EGU26 abstracts. Only one GRACE TWSA diffusion paper was found: **Kiani Shahvandi (2026), Deep-GRASP, Geodesy and Geodynamics, doi:10.1016/j.geog.2026.09.010**, with an earlier ESSOAr preprint doi:10.22541/essoar.15007597/v1. It is diamond OA (CC BY-NC-ND), but ScienceDirect and ESSOAr both returned 403 or a Cloudflare challenge to automated fetching, and the Elsevier text-mining API refused the request. The full text could NOT be read, so the method details below come from the abstract only. An EGU26 abstract (EGU26-5255) applies DDPMs to soil-moisture downscaling, which is not GRACE.

## Claims in the preliminary review (preliminary_review_2026-11-12.md): verification and corrections

**Confirmed against full text:**
- He 2021: RMSEs of 22.3 (SRCNN), 14.4 (VDSR), 18.4 (RCAN) and 82.0 mm (bicubic, exactly 82.02); 158 months of data.
- Gou & Soja 2024: 21.9 mm versus 49.2 mm over 288 basins.
- Xiong 2026a: median RMSE of 1.87 (Gou & Soja), 2.24 (SYSU) and 3.40 cm (GLWS), and the "GRACE-constrained reconstruction informed by high-resolution spatial priors" quote.
- Suryawanshi 2026: Gou & Soja improves in 16 mascons and the mascon-wise PLSR variant in 17.
- Pascal 2022: R 0.76 → 0.79 with 1006 wells.
- Mo 2021/2022 use particle-based SVGD (20 particles), so "particle ensembles" is correct.
- Uz 2026: variational U-Net plus MC dropout, 95th-percentile uncertainty about 3.2–3.3 cm.
- D-SHIFT: nESRGAN+ generator with a U-Net discriminator, 2008/2015/2016 held out for tuning (not a strict test set), and the authors report high-frequency noise and isolated daily excursions.
- RecGAN: NSE 0.17 → 0.46 in the Nelson basin.
- Yin 2023: random train/test split.

**Corrections and flags:**
1. **He 2021 training-set size.** The review says "640 training tiles from 158 months". The paper reports 42,660 tiles in total (38,394 training and 4,266 validation). The figure of 640 (and 64) tiles appears in a separate sentence whose context I did not resolve, possibly for a reduced experiment. Treat "640 tiles" as unverified or likely incorrect.
2. **He 2021 baseline.** The abstract's headline baseline is linear-regression downscaling (71.6 mm). Bicubic (82.02 mm) is a secondary baseline. "Against 82.0 mm for bicubic" is not wrong but omits the main baseline.
3. **Mo et al. 2025** is described as "forecast TWSA one to three months ahead". It is nowcasting over the product latency, and it uses hydrometeorological forcing for the target months as input. It is not a pure forecast.
4. **GRAiCE** is described as "BiLSTM-based". It provides four reconstructions from both LSTM and BiLSTM.
5. **Section 4.2 ("Three approaches give uncertainty").** This omits GRACE-REC's 100-member statistical ensembles, Gou & Soja's deep ensembles, Li 2026's reported median uncertainty of 0.88 cm, and Kiani Shahvandi's probabilistic PINN. The conclusion (per-pixel spread, no coherent samples) still holds.
6. **Fan et al. 2025 (SSRN).** The title says "novel deep learning model incorporating spatiotemporal features", not GAN. Listing it among "nine GAN papers" is an inference from keywords; I could not confirm it.
7. **Scope gaps.** The review omits the learned-correction line (Piretzidis 2018, Zhang Q. 2023, Chen W. 2025 JSTARS), the forecasting literature (Steidl 2026, Li F. 2025, Kiani Shahvandi 2026 PINN, Mo 2025), the gap-filling literature (Uz 2022, Lai 2022, Zhang B. 2022, Shi 2024, Wang 2024 GAIN, Gyawali 2022), reconstructions (DeepRec 2025, Mandal 2025, Sun Z. 2020, Li F. 2020/2021, Sun A. 2021 AutoML) and mass-conserving tree baselines (Wu 2026). These are not errors, given its stated scope.
8. **Deep-GRASP** remains readable only through its abstract. The finding that it is the only GRACE diffusion paper holds: my independent search (OpenAlex, arXiv API, GFZ list grep, web search) found no other.
9. **Zhang 2026 High Plains claim.** The review says a "trend-sign mismatch in the High Plains Aquifer is reported for a 1 km product". This is confirmed in the full text for 2003–2013.
10. **Points I could not check:** the referee criticisms of Li 2026 (open-review files not read), Uz 2026's Alaska and Nile trend numbers (only partial trend text was extracted), and the Goracci 14.47 → 7.10 mm figure (it appears in the text, but I did not confirm that it refers to the Danube).

**Errors in the task brief (for the writer):**
- "Sun et al. 2019 'Predicting GRACE TWSA with DL' WRR" has the wrong title. The correct one is "Combining physically based modeling and deep learning for fusing GRACE satellite data: Can we learn from mismatch?".
- "Li et al. 2021 Nature Communications" was not found. The likely intended paper is Li F. et al. 2021 GRL (doi:10.1029/2021GL093492).
- "Gerdener" is cited for downscaling, but Gerdener et al. 2023 (GLWS2.0, J. Geod., doi:10.1007/s00190-023-01763-9) is data assimilation, not ML.
