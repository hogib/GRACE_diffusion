## Summary of the sub-field

**Taxonomy of applications.** Generative models for gridded geophysical fields cluster into six task families. Each maps onto a GRACE problem.
(1) *Statistical downscaling/super-resolution* of reanalysis or climate-model output: CorrDiff, R2-D2, Tomasi, Addison, Watt & Mansfield, Ling, Hess, Aich, Bochow, Ribeiro, WassDiff, STVD. The GRACE analogue is mascon or SH fields to 0.25° or finer.
(2) *Probabilistic forecasting and ensemble emulation*: GenCast, SEEDS, FuXi-ENS, ArchesWeatherGen, StormCast, and the nowcasting models DGMR, LDCast, PreDiff and CasCast. The analogues are TWS prediction and ensemble UQ.
(3) *Data assimilation and reconstruction from sparse observations*: SDA, DiffDA, Appa, Manshausen, GenDA (Martin), Chakraborty, Qu, Asefi, SAGE, GenCRest. The analogues are bridging the 2017–2018 GRACE/GRACE-FO gap, reconstructing pre-2002 TWS, and fusing wells, GNSS and altimetry.
(4) *Gap filling and inpainting* of satellite products: Barth (chlorophyll), DIRECT (SST), Kishikawa (precipitation), DiffCR (cloud removal), MBES-DDPM (bathymetry swaths), Hou (gravity/magnetic survey grids).
(5) *Hydrology-specific* time-series diffusion: DRUM, h-Diffusion, HydroDiffusion, Bhavsar, and Jiao for groundwater plumes.
(6) *Climate emulators and foundation models*: DiffESM, ACE/ACE2, Spherical DYffusion, cBottle.
I found no diffusion paper on GRACE TWS in this sub-field's literature. The GRACE-specific generative work (GANs, Deep-GRASP) is covered by the dl_grace agent.

**Architectures.** Most models are U-Nets with attention in the EDM parameterisation of Karras et al. (CorrDiff, StormCast, cBottle, Manshausen), or DDPM U-Nets (Ling, Han, Barth, Singh, Hou). Latent diffusion appears in LDCast, PreDiff, Tomasi and Appa (565M parameters, global 0.25° hourly). Transformers and graph processors appear in GenCast (graph-transformer on an icosahedral mesh), SEEDS (axial transformer), CasCast and Bhavsar. Flow matching or rectified flow is increasingly used and is often equal or better: DIRECT and the Rampal intercomparison, where flow matching had the best calibration. Consistency models are used for few-step sampling (Hess 2025; Bochow 2026). The dominant downscaling design is **residual (corrective) diffusion**. A deterministic regressor, or a physical model in R2-D2, predicts the conditional mean, and diffusion samples only the residual. CorrDiff introduced this because directly conditioned diffusion "showed slow convergence and resulted in poor-quality images with incoherent structures" over a 12.5× ratio. Tomasi (LDM_res), STVD (Srivastava) and R2-D2 (residual after cubic interpolation of the 45 km RCM field) follow the same pattern.

**Conditioning approaches.** There are four.
(a) *Supervised conditional training* on paired coarse/fine data, usually by channel concatenation: CorrDiff, Addison, Ling, Han, Singh.
(b) *Cross-attention to an encoder*. Ribeiro found that concatenation gives the best pointwise CRPS but over-smooths extremes, while cross-attention gives better distributions and tails. A frozen Prithvi-WxC encoder matched full-data skill with only five years of training data.
(c) *Unconditional prior plus inference-time guidance*. This includes SDEdit-style partial noising (Bischoff & Deck; Hess, with the noise level chosen where the coarse and fine PSDs intersect; Aich, with a shared embedding space) and posterior sampling with an explicit observation operator: DPS or MMPS in SDA, DiffDA, Manshausen, GenDA, Tie, SAGE and GenCRest, and pseudo-inverse guidance in Forestier.
(d) *Inpainting or observation-anchored sampling* for gaps: Barth, DIRECT, h-Diffusion-DA, Kishikawa.
Suresh Babu et al. compared (a) and (c) directly on QG turbulence. Conditional models reconstruct fine scales and tails and give spread that predicts error. DPS gives reasonable but smoothed fields, SDEdit gives unphysical fields, and neither guided method propagates information into unobserved regions. Tie et al. show the converse for domain shift. Supervised models (UNet, CorrDiff) trained on ERA5 pairs "deteriorate significantly ... yielding results even inferior to the coarse input" on real GCM input, while zero-shot DPS with an area-weighted averaging operator generalises.

**Evaluation practice.** The standard suite is: CRPS and spread–skill ratio or rank histograms (GenCast, CorrDiff, R2-D2, Manshausen, Barth's CRPS decomposition); radially averaged power spectra and variograms (nearly all papers); and distributional and extreme metrics such as Q–Q plots, 99th percentiles, return levels and the Wasserstein distance in WassDiff. Some papers add multivariate correlations (R2-D2), climate-change-signal fidelity (Rampal 2025, CORDEX-ML-Bench, Addison) and cross-region generalisation (Singh). Calibration is the recurring weak point. CorrDiff ensembles are "under-dispersive for most channels" and R2-D2 is "slightly underdispersive" (spread–skill 0.66–0.80). In Rampal 2025 GANs are under-dispersive, while diffusion and flow matching are better calibrated. Singh found across-seed variability larger than within-seed sampling variability for exceedance probabilities, so a single trained model's ensemble understates total uncertainty.

**Reported gains over deterministic baselines.** These papers agree that deterministic U-Nets regress to the mean, damp high-wavenumber power and underestimate extremes. Generative models restore spectra and tails: CorrDiff (kinetic energy at 10–200 km, radar reflectivity), Tomasi (LDM beats a GAN with the same U-Net backbone), Singh, Rampal and WassDiff. Gains are largest for intermittent, heavy-tailed fields such as precipitation. They are small for smooth fields such as temperature (CorrDiff: tails "only incrementally improved"; CORDEX-ML-Bench: "the generative advantage narrows" for temperature). Pointwise RMSE is often equal or slightly worse. Forestier's diffusion SSS downscaling is "comparable to a strong convolutional baseline" in RMSE/SSIM, and Ribeiro's concatenation model wins on CRPS but loses tails. TWS is smooth, so the case for diffusion on GRACE rests mainly on spatially coherent ensembles and UQ, physically plausible fine structure and robustness to domain shift. Large RMSE gains should not be expected.

**Computational cost.** Diffusion inference costs 10–1000 network evaluations per sample. In Tomasi's table, training takes about 870–1100 GPU-hours for the LDM against about 250–380 for the U-Net. Inference is about 15 GPU-hours per test year against about 1. Singh concluded that DDPM's gains "are generally small relative to its substantially greater computational cost". Ways to reduce cost include higher-order samplers (Rampal: 25 steps with Adams–Bashforth 2 at almost no skill loss), consistency models (Hess, Bochow: 1 to a few steps) and latent diffusion (Appa, LDCast). GRACE grids are small (0.25° global is 720×1440, with about 250 monthly samples), so cost matters less than data scarcity.

**Lessons for GRACE.**
(i) **Residual or corrective diffusion** on top of a deterministic downscaler or a GRACE-constrained hydrological prior: CorrDiff, R2-D2, Tomasi.
(ii) **Mass conservation must be hard, not soft.** Harder et al. show that constraint layers (AddCL, MultCL, SmCL) enforce coarse-cell means exactly and usually improve RMSE. Soft penalties leave violations and can worsen RMSE. Bochow et al. apply such a constraint *during diffusion/consistency-model inference* without retraining. Tie et al. use an area-weighted averaging operator inside DPS guidance. Projected diffusion (Christopher) and ECI sampling (Cheng) give general hard-constraint machinery for linear operators. For GRACE the operator is not a block mean. It is SH truncation plus filtering, or a mascon averaging kernel with correlated errors, but it is still linear, so projection or guidance applies directly.
(iii) **Sparse and heterogeneous observation conditioning without retraining** via SDA/DPS. Examples are Manshausen (a few dozen stations), GenDA (SSH+SST to unobserved currents), SAGE (satellite plus in situ, progressive fusion) and Chakraborty (ERA5 coarse plus radiosondes). This enables fusion of wells, GNSS loading and altimetry with GRACE.
(iv) **Training on incomplete data**, as in Barth and DIRECT, matters because no gap-free fine-scale TWS truth exists.
(v) **Small-sample regimes**: Addison reports good climatology from 14 years of training data, and Ribeiro's foundation-model encoder works with 5 years. Both use daily data, which is far more samples than about 250 GRACE months.
(vi) **Domain shift** from model-trained priors to real observations is the main failure mode (Tie, Rampal, Forestier's stated next steps). Validate on independent observations, as Han did with along-track altimetry.
(vii) **Calibrate ensembles** and report spread–skill, and use multiple training seeds (Singh).
(viii) **Residual designs overfit when data are scarce.** ArchesWeatherGen found that overfitting of the deterministic base model is the main obstacle. Training the residual model on data the base model has not seen fixes it. This is critical with about 250 GRACE months.
(ix) **Latent diffusion smooths fine scales.** Finn et al. (sea ice) and Appa's authors both report this. Given GRACE's small grids, data-space diffusion is affordable and preferable.

### A. Downscaling and super-resolution with diffusion

**mardani2025corrdiff**. Mardani, M., Brenowitz, N., Cohen, Y., Pathak, J., Chen, C.-Y., Liu, C.-C., Vahdat, A., Nabian, M. A., Ge, T., Subramaniam, A., Kashinath, K., Kautz, J., Pritchard, M. (2025). Residual corrective diffusion modeling for km-scale atmospheric downscaling. *Communications Earth & Environment* 6, 124. DOI 10.1038/s43247-025-02042-5; arXiv:2309.15214. *Weather downscaling, ERA5 25 km to 2 km WRF over Taiwan (12.5×).*
A UNet predicts the conditional mean and an EDM diffusion model samples the residual. Training used 3 years (2018–2020, about 24k samples), with 2021 as the test year. The model recovers kinetic-energy spectra at 10–200 km and radar-reflectivity distributions, and synthesises typhoon rain bands. Temperature tails are only incrementally improved, and the 32-member ensembles are under-dispersive for most channels. This is the reference design for residual diffusion on a GRACE deterministic downscaler. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Mardani2025_CorrDiff.pdf, arXiv v4).

**lopezgomez2025r2d2**. Lopez-Gomez, I., Wan, Z. Y., Zepeda-Núñez, L., Schneider, T., Anderson, J., Sha, F. (2025). Dynamical-generative downscaling of climate model ensembles. *PNAS* 122, e2420288122. DOI 10.1073/pnas.2420288122; arXiv:2410.01776. *Climate-ensemble downscaling (R2-D2).*
A WRF regional model downscales CMIP6 ESMs to 45 km. A diffusion model then generates the residual between the 9 km fields and cubic-interpolated 45 km fields. CRPS falls by more than 40% relative to BCSD for all fields, and spectra and multivariate correlations improve. Ensembles are slightly under-dispersive, with spread–skill 0.66 for temperature and 0.80 for precipitation. The hybrid "physics then generative" chain suggests GRACE-constrained hydrological-model output followed by generative refinement. **FULLTEXT_READ** (LopezGomez2025_DynGenDownscaling.pdf, arXiv v1; claims checked in arXiv HTML and PDF text).

**tomasi2025ldm**. Tomasi, E., Franch, G., Cristoforetti, M. (2025). Can AI be enabled to perform dynamical downscaling? A latent diffusion model to mimic kilometer-scale COSMO5.0_CLM9 simulations. *Geosci. Model Dev.* 18, 2051. DOI 10.5194/gmd-18-2051-2025. *ERA5 to 2 km over Italy (2 m temperature, 10 m wind).*
A residual latent diffusion model on top of a U-Net beats quadratic interpolation, the U-Net and a GAN with the same U-Net backbone in spatial error, distributions and power spectra. The paper reports GPU-hours: about 870–1100 to train the LDM against about 250–380 for the U-Net, and about 15 against about 1 for one test year of inference. Variables are trained separately, so inter-variable consistency is not guaranteed. This is useful cost evidence for the GRACE design. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Tomasi2025_LDM_COSMO.pdf).

**ling2024dpdm**. Ling, F., Lu, Z., Luo, J.-J., Bai, L., Behera, S. K., Jin, D., Pan, B., Jiang, H., Yamagata, T. (2024). Diffusion model-based probabilistic downscaling for 180-year East Asian climate reconstruction. arXiv:2402.06646. *1° to 0.1° (10×) downscaling of 20CR, monthly products 1836–2015.*
A DDPM downscaler gives ensembles for UQ and a 180-year monthly dataset. **Note:** training used **6-hourly data from 1961–2015** with random patch sampling. The authors assume the coarse–fine mapping is scale-invariant across time scales and apply the model to monthly means. The model therefore was not trained on a monthly-sized sample. The trick is still relevant to GRACE: train on a higher-frequency proxy such as daily model TWS and apply to monthly fields. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Ling2024_DPDM_EastAsia.pdf).

**ribeiro2026prithvi**. Ribeiro, V. N., Guevara, J., Moraga, J. S., Lucas, C., Lord, N., Taylor, A., et al. (2026). Precipitation downscaling using foundation model-conditioned diffusion. arXiv:2608.25858. *Daily precipitation, Colorado River Basin.*
This paper compares concatenation, a learned-encoder cross-attention and a frozen Prithvi-WxC cross-attention. Concatenation has the lowest CRPS/MSE but over-smooths, giving almost no >100 mm/day events. Cross-attention gives distribution biases up to 5× lower and better spectra. The Prithvi-conditioned model matches full-data performance with 5 years of training. For GRACE this argues for conditioning design and pretrained encoders in data-poor settings. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Ribeiro2026_FMConditionedDiffusion.pdf).

**bischoff2024bridges**. Bischoff, T., Deck, K. (2024). Unpaired downscaling of fluid flows with diffusion bridges. *Artificial Intelligence for the Earth Systems*. DOI 10.1175/AIES-D-23-0039.1; arXiv:2305.01822. *Idealised fluid downscaling without paired data.*
The method chains two independently trained diffusion models. Source (low-res) samples are diffused forward "until small-scale information is lost", at a time chosen from the Fourier spectra, and then reverse-diffused with the high-res model. It corrects context-dependent biases including extremes. This gives an unpaired option for GRACE, since no true fine-scale TWS exists. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Bischoff2024_DiffusionBridges.pdf, arXiv v1; the AIES PDF download was blocked).

**hess2025scaleadaptive**. Hess, P., Aich, M., Pan, B., Boers, N. (2025). Fast, scale-adaptive and uncertainty-aware downscaling of Earth system model fields with generative machine learning. *Nature Machine Intelligence* 7, 363–373. DOI 10.1038/s42256-025-00980-5; arXiv:2403.02774. *ESM precipitation to ERA5 resolution (4×) with a consistency model.*
A consistency model trained only on ERA5 noises the ESM field to a time t* set where the ESM and ERA5 PSDs intersect (k* = 0.0667, t* = 0.468), then denoises in one step. No explicit physical constraint is used. The global mean is "only approximately conserved", and the authors suggest hard constraints. The SI gives t* = 0.63, which conflicts with the Methods value. A PSD-intersection rule suits band-limited GRACE. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Hess2025_ScaleAdaptiveDownscaling.pdf; arXiv v4 HTML read).

**aich2026conddiff**. Aich, M., Hess, P., Pan, B., Bathiany, S., Huang, Y., Boers, N. (2026). Conditional diffusion models for downscaling and bias correction of Earth system model precipitation. *Geosci. Model Dev.* 19, 1791. DOI 10.5194/gmd-19-1791-2026. *Joint bias correction and downscaling.*
Observations and ESM fields are mapped to a shared embedding space by smoothing above a chosen correction scale, where they are unbiased relative to each other. A conditional diffusion model trained on observations alone reverses the mapping. Large-scale patterns above the correction scale are preserved, which is a "spectral" consistency rather than a hard mass constraint. The model outperforms statistical and DL baselines on extremes. This maps well onto GRACE, which trusts scales above about 300 km and needs generation below that. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Aich2026_CondDiffusionBiasCorr.pdf; abstract, method and conservation passages checked).

**bochow2026smb**. Bochow, N., Hess, P., Robinson, A. (2026). Physics-constrained generative machine learning-based high-resolution downscaling of Greenland's surface mass balance and surface temperature. *The Cryosphere* 20, 1841. DOI 10.5194/tc-20-1841-2026. *Ice-sheet SMB downscaling, 160 km to 5 km (32×).*
A consistency model trained on monthly MARv3.12 output is conditioned on topography and insolation. A **hard conservation constraint is applied during inference**: superblock pooling after Harder et al., no retraining. It approximately preserves coarse-scale SMB sums and generalises to extreme climates. CRPS is 5.37 mm w.e./month for SMB. This is the closest published analogue to a mass-conserving generative GRACE downscaler, since it uses monthly mass fields from a glaciological model. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Bochow2026_GreenlandSMB.pdf).

**tie2026zssd**. Tie, R., Xiong, W., Shi, Z., Su, X., Jiang, C., Wu, L., et al. (2026). Zero-shot statistical downscaling via diffusion posterior sampling. arXiv:2601.21760. *ERA5-trained prior applied to 5 CMIP6 GCMs (×6 to ×20).*
A prior is conditioned on geophysical boundaries and time. "Unified Coordinate Guidance" uses an operator A = A_high∘A_low, where A_low is an area-weighted average to 5° that the paper says conserves mass and energy. This addresses vanishing DPS gradients at large scale factors. In App. B.1, supervised baselines including CorrDiff trained on paired ERA5 degrade on IPSL "even inferior to the coarse input" (MAE 1.16–1.32 against 1.06 for bilinear). This is directly transferable: put the GRACE averaging kernel in A_low. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Tie2026_ZeroShotDPS.pdf; HTML v2 checked).

**chakraborty2025multimodal**. Chakraborty, D., Guan, H., Stock, J., Arcomano, T., Cervone, G., Maulik, R. (2025). Multimodal atmospheric super-resolution with deep generative models. DOI 10.1088/3049-4753/ae286e; arXiv:2506.22780. *Score-based zero-shot fusion of coarse ERA5 and IGRA radiosondes.*
An unconditional score model on ERA5 is conditioned at inference on coarse grids and/or sparse radiosondes in a Bayesian formulation. The model balances the modalities and gives uncertainty estimates. This is a template for fusing GRACE with sparse wells or GNSS. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Chakraborty2025_MultimodalSR.pdf). The journal name for the DOI was not confirmed.

**han2024ssh**. Han, Q., Jiang, X., Zhao, Y., Wang, X., Li, Z., Zhang, R. (2024). Generative diffusion model-based downscaling of observed sea surface height over Kuroshio Extension since 2000. arXiv:2408.12632. *SSH 0.25° to 1/16°.*
KESHDiff is a **conditional** DDPM: the low-resolution SSH is fed to the U-Net in the reverse process. It is trained on GLORYS12 reanalysis, applied to observed AVISO SSH and validated against along-track altimeter spectra. It reports intensified EKE below 250 km since 2004. This gives a sim-to-observation transfer recipe with independent validation, which GRACE needs (e.g., with wells or GNSS). **FULLTEXT_READ** (Han2024_SSHDiffusionDownscaling.pdf).

**forestier2026sss**. Forestier, E., Ollier, L., El Hourany, R., Boutin, J., Mejia, C., Thiria, S. (2026). Sea surface salinity downscaling using deep generative diffusion models. *Ocean Science* 22, 2357–2373. DOI 10.5194/os-22-2357-2026. *SSS 1/3° to 1/12° with SST/SSH auxiliaries (GLORYS, Gulf Stream).*
A multichannel unconditional prior is guided at inference by pseudo-inverse guidance toward the coarse SSS, together with high-resolution SST and/or SSH. Accuracy is comparable to a strong CNN (RESAC) under RMSE/SSIM, and the model adds an ensemble. Calibration of the ensemble spread is explicitly not established. The authors list product-aware observation operators, uncertainty weighting and in situ validation as prerequisites for real data. This is the closest structural analogue to GRACE plus auxiliary high-resolution predictors. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Forestier2026_SSSDiffusion.pdf).

**sureshbabu2025guided**. Suresh Babu, A. N., Sadam, A., Lermusiaux, P. F. J. (2026). Guided unconditional and conditional generative models for super-resolution and inference of quasi-geostrophic turbulence. *JAMES* 18, e2025MS005324. DOI 10.1029/2025MS005324; arXiv:2507.00719. *Comparison of SDEdit, DPS, conditional and CFG diffusion.*
Conditional models (vanilla and CFG) are accurate and cycle-consistent with observations, reproduce spectra and tails, and give errors predictable from ensemble std. DPS is smoothed, and SDEdit is unphysical and fails on sparse or gappy data. Neither guided method propagates information to unobserved regions. This is the key evidence for the trade-off between paired-conditional and guided designs on GRACE. **FULLTEXT_READ** (SureshBabu2025_GuidedVsConditionalQG.pdf).

**addison2026cpmgem**. Addison, H., Kendon, E., Ravuri, S., Aitchison, L., Watson, P. A. G. (2026). Machine learning emulation of precipitation from km-scale UK regional climate simulations using a diffusion model. *JAMES*. DOI 10.1029/2025MS005140; arXiv:2407.14158. *60 km GCM variables to 8.8 km daily precipitation (CPMGEM).*
Samples have realistic extremes up to about 100-year return periods. The stochastic component is "reasonably well-calibrated". The summer climate-change signal is captured, with some error in other seasons. Trained on about 500 years, but good climatology is obtained with only 14 years of training data. This is relevant to GRACE's limited record. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Addison2024_UKPrecipDiffusion.pdf, arXiv version).

**watt2024diffdownscaling**. Watt, R. A., Mansfield, L. A. (2024). Generative diffusion-based downscaling for climate. arXiv:2404.17752. *ERA5 2° to 0.25°.*
A diffusion model outperforms a U-Net with the same architecture. The authors note non-stationarity and coarse/fine mismatch as open issues. This is a minimal baseline study. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Watt2024_GenDiffDownscaling.pdf).

**wan2023debias**. Wan, Z. Y., Baptista, R., Chen, Y., Anderson, J., Boral, A., Sha, F., Zepeda-Núñez, L. (2023). Debias coarsely, sample conditionally: statistical downscaling through optimal transport and probabilistic diffusion models. NeurIPS 36 (venue confirmed from the PDF footer). arXiv:2305.15618. *Unpaired downscaling of 1D/2D fluid flows, 8× to 16×.*
OT debiasing is followed by diffusion upsampling with a-posteriori conditional sampling. It works when the low-frequency content of input and output differ. This is relevant when GRACE and model TWS disagree at large scales. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Wan2023_DebiasCoarsely.pdf).

**srivastava2024stvd**. Srivastava, P., Yang, R., Kerrigan, G., Dresdner, G., McGibbon, J., Bretherton, C., Mandt, S. (2024). Precipitation downscaling with spatiotemporal video diffusion. arXiv:2312.06071. *Video (temporal) residual diffusion.*
A deterministic video SR network is followed by diffusion on residual details, which gives temporally coherent fine-scale precipitation. The authors flag paired-data dependence and distribution shift as limitations. This suits GRACE monthly sequences. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Srivastava2024_SpatiotemporalVideoDiffusion.pdf).

**liu2025wassdiff**. Liu, Y., Doss-Gollin, J., Dai, Q., Veeraraghavan, A., Balakrishnan, G. (2025). Downscaling extreme precipitation with Wasserstein regularized diffusion. *IEEE TGRS* 63, 4110211. DOI 10.1109/TGRS.2025.3611872; arXiv:2410.00381. *CPC gauges plus ERA5 (55 km) to 1 km radar-quality precipitation.*
A score-based model with a Wasserstein distribution-matching regulariser improves tails and calibration in Q–Q plots. Tiled diffusion extends it to continental CONUS. This is relevant as a "gauge-plus-reanalysis" fusion and for the distribution-matching loss. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Liu2025_WassDiff.pdf).

**pathak2026stormcast**. Pathak, J., Cohen, Y., Garg, P., Harrington, P., Brenowitz, N., Durran, D., Mardani, M., Vahdat, A., Xu, S., Kashinath, K., Pritchard, M. (2026). Kilometer-scale convection-allowing model emulation using generative diffusion modeling. *Science Advances*. DOI 10.1126/sciadv.adv0423; arXiv:2408.10958. *Autoregressive emulation of 3 km HRRR (99 variables, dense boundary-layer levels, 1 h steps) conditioned on 26 ERA5 synoptic variables.*
The paper reports four findings. Forecasts are competitive with the operational HRRR. Power spectra and distributions validate well. A 5-member ensemble's probability-matched mean beats a single physics-based forecast. The model stays robust when ERA5 conditioning is swapped for GFS forecasts at test time. The authors also note that km-scale regional training data are "under-sampled in time" compared with global reanalyses, which is the same data-scarcity issue GRACE faces. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Pathak2024_StormCast.pdf).

**rampal2025intercomparison**. Rampal, N., Ward-Leikis, B., Koh, Y. S., Gibson, P. B., Liu, H.-Y., Kitsios, V., et al. (2025). An intercomparison of generative machine learning methods for downscaling precipitation at fine spatial scales. arXiv:2512.13987. *GANs vs diffusion vs flow matching, New Zealand RCM.*
The deterministic baseline regresses to the mean. Diffusion and flow matching give better-calibrated ensembles than GANs, which are under-dispersive, and flow matching has the best calibration. A second-order sampler cuts steps to about 25. All but two models underestimate climate-change signals of extremes, so "visual realism" and standard metrics do not guarantee skill on change signals. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Rampal2025_GenIntercomparison.pdf).

**rampal2026cordexml**. Rampal, N., González-Abad, J., Addison, H., Baño-Medina, J., Bettolli, M. L., Blasone, V., et al. (2026). CORDEX-ML-Bench: a benchmark for data-driven regional climate downscaling, experiment design and overview. arXiv:2606.29172. *Community benchmark, 40 ML configurations, 3 regions, about 10 km.*
Generative models consistently beat deterministic ones for precipitation, but "the generative advantage narrows" for temperature. Models trained on the historical period only underestimate future signals. Relevant to GRACE because TWS is smooth like temperature. Submitted to JAMES. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Rampal2026_CORDEXMLBench.pdf; abstract and conclusions).

**singh2026intercomparison**. Singh, S., Papalexiou, S. M., Abdelmoaty, H. M., Hartvigsen, T., Mamalakis, A. (2026). Comprehensive inter-comparison of generative AI models for super-resolution precipitation downscaling across hydroclimatic regimes. *Geosci. Model Dev.* 19, 7545–7567. DOI 10.5194/gmd-19-7545-2026. *U-Net vs WGAN vs DDPM, 8× and 16×, cross-region test.*
Generative models beat the U-Net on distributions and spatial structure. WGAN is best for exceedance probabilities at lower cost. DDPM best reproduces autocorrelation and spectra, but its gains "are generally small relative to its substantially greater computational cost". Across-seed variability exceeds within-seed sampling variability for exceedance probabilities; the difference is less pronounced for spectra. For GRACE, train multiple seeds. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Singh2026_GenIntercomparison.pdf).

**tu2025mods**. Tu, S., Xu, J., Yang, W., Bai, L., Fei, B. (2025). MODS: multi-source observations conditional diffusion model for meteorological state downscaling. arXiv:2506.14798. *ERA5 downscaling conditioned on multiple satellites.*
Features from polar-orbiting sounders (AMSU-A, HIRS, MHS), geostationary GridSat and GEBCO topography pass through a pretrained encoder and are fused by cross-attention. At sampling time, low-resolution ERA5 and station observations act together as "multi-guidance". This is a template for conditioning on heterogeneous high-resolution auxiliaries plus point observations. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Tu2025_MODS.pdf).

**zhang2025pgdm**. Zhang, H., Tang, B.-H., Hu, T., Jiang, Y., Li, Z.-L. (2025). PGDM: physically guided diffusion model for land surface temperature downscaling. arXiv:2511.05964. *LST downscaling.*
Downscaling is posed as sampling the posterior of high-resolution LST given low-resolution LST and a suite of high-resolution geophysical priors, motivated by the surface energy balance. The training set has 22,909 patches over China, with external tests in 40 regions worldwide. The model beats interpolation, kernel-driven, hybrid and DL methods. The ensemble std correlates strongly and linearly with actual error, so the model can self-assess its uncertainty. The paper also analyses the upscaling operator: simple averaging versus energy conservation with emissivity. This parallels the need to define the GRACE aggregation operator correctly. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Zhang2025_PGDM.pdf).

### B. GAN-era generative downscaling (baselines)

**leinonen2021gan**. Leinonen, J., Nerini, D., Berne, A. (2021). Stochastic super-resolution for downscaling time-evolving atmospheric fields with a generative adversarial network. *IEEE TGRS* 59(9), 7211–7223. DOI 10.1109/TGRS.2020.3032790; arXiv:2005.10374. A recurrent conditional GAN gives ensembles of temporally consistent SR sequences for Swiss radar precipitation and GOES-16 cloud optical thickness. Rank statistics show "close to the correct amount of variability". The fully convolutional generator applies to larger domains and longer series than it was trained on. This is the early GAN reference for stochastic SR. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Leinonen2021_GANDownscaling.pdf).

**harris2022gan**. Harris, L., McRae, A. T. T., Chantry, M., Dueben, P. D., Palmer, T. N. (2022). A generative deep learning approach to stochastic downscaling of precipitation forecasts. *JAMES*. DOI 10.1029/2022MS003120; arXiv:2204.02028. A GAN and a VAE-GAN correct and downscale (10×) IFS forecasts against radar. The VAE-GAN has slightly better CRPS. The GAN is better calibrated and produces more intense rainfall. Both are comparable to the operational ecPoint, which still gives the best-calibrated rank histograms. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Harris2022_GANDownscaling.pdf).

**hess2022pcgan**. Hess, P., Drüke, M., Petri, S., Strnad, F., Boers, N. (2022). Physically constrained generative adversarial networks for improving precipitation fields from Earth system models. *Nature Machine Intelligence* 4, 828. DOI 10.1038/s42256-022-00540-1; arXiv:2209.07568. A GAN post-processes ESM precipitation with a constraint layer that reproduces the ESM's global mean "by construction" while improving regional patterns. The authors note that a conserved quantity "cannot be improved with respect to observations by definition of the constraint". The GRACE lesson is to constrain to the *observed* GRACE large-scale mass, not to a model's. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Hess2022_PhysConstrainedGAN.pdf; abstract and constraint passages).

**price2022precipgen**. Price, I., Rasp, S. (2022). Increasing the accuracy and resolution of precipitation forecasts using deep generative models. AISTATS 2022 (venue confirmed from the PDF footer). arXiv:2203.12297. CorrectorGAN has two stages: map the coarse forecast to a corrected distribution, then generate high-resolution samples, using radar as truth over CONUS. It matches or beats interpolation, SR-GAN and CNN baselines on probabilistic metrics. It is close to an operational high-resolution regional model and more reliable for extreme rain. This is a "correct then downscale" design, analogous to correcting GRACE leakage before downscaling. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Price2022_PrecipGenerative.pdf).

### C. Physical constraints and conservation

**harder2023hard**. Harder, P., Hernandez-Garcia, A., Ramesh, V., Yang, Q., Sattigeri, P., Szwarcman, D., Watson, C., Rolnick, D. (2023). Hard-constrained deep learning for climate downscaling. *JMLR* 24(365), 1–40 (confirmed on jmlr.org). arXiv:2208.05424.
The paper proposes four constraint layers (AddCL, ScAddCL, MultCL, SmCL) that enforce coarse-cell means exactly. Hard constraints usually improve RMSE. Soft penalties suffer an "accuracy-constraints trade-off": for example, RMSE rose from 0.657 to 0.801 for the TCW4 CNN. The exception is NorESM temperature, where coarse and fine come from separate model runs with large constraint violation and the unconstrained CNN was best. This is the core reference for GRACE mass conservation. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Harder2023_HardConstrained.pdf; arXiv v9 HTML read).

**christopher2024projected**. Christopher, J. K., Baek, S., Fioretto, F. (2024). Constrained synthesis with projected diffusion models. NeurIPS 2024 (venue confirmed from the PDF footer). arXiv:2402.03559. Sampling is recast as constrained optimisation with repeated projections, giving certified compliance for convex constraints and empirical success on non-convex and ODE constraints (materials, motion, planning). For a linear GRACE averaging operator the projection is exact and cheap. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Christopher2024_ProjectedDiffusion.pdf; abstract and conclusions).

**cheng2025eci**. Cheng, C., Han, B., Maddix, D. C., Ansari, A. F., Stuart, A., Mahoney, M. W., Wang, Y. (2025). Gradient-free generation for hard-constrained systems. ICLR 2025 (venue confirmed from the PDF header). arXiv:2412.01786. ECI sampling alternates extrapolation, correction and interpolation in each flow-matching step. It enforces hard constraints *exactly* and zero-shot on pretrained, unconstrained models, without gradients. The paper names mass and energy conservation as motivating cases and demonstrates the method on PDE systems. Its limitation is task-specific tuning of the number of mixing iterations and the resampling interval. A conservation correction step with the GRACE averaging operator fits this framework directly. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Cheng2025_ECIsampling.pdf).

**jacobsen2023cocogen**. Jacobsen, C., Zhuang, Y., Duraisamy, K. (2023). CoCoGen: physically-consistent and conditioned score-based generative models for forward and inverse problems. arXiv:2312.10527. Conditional score models plus "physical consistency sampling" steps, which are gradient steps on the PDE residual during sampling. Sampling with the probability-flow ODE gives smaller residuals than the reverse SDE. Residuals are small across applications, but the step-size hyperparameter τ needs tuning. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Jacobsen2023_CoCoGen.pdf).

**bastek2025pidm**. Bastek, J.-H., Sun, W., Kochmann, D. M. (2025). Physics-informed diffusion models. ICLR 2025 (venue from the PDF header). arXiv:2403.14404. A first-principles loss term during *training* (a virtual-likelihood view) penalises PDE residuals of the denoised estimate. It greatly reduces residuals relative to purely data-driven diffusion, with evidence of less overfitting. This is the training-time counterpart to inference-time constraints. A water-balance residual such as ΔTWS − (P − ET − Q) could serve as such a loss. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Bastek2025_PIDM.pdf).

**shu2023pidiffusion**. Shu, D., Li, Z., Barati Farimani, A. (2023). A physics-informed diffusion model for high-fidelity flow field reconstruction. *J. Comput. Phys.*, DOI 10.1016/j.jcp.2023.111972; arXiv:2211.14680. A diffusion model trained *only on high-fidelity data* reconstructs from low-fidelity or sparsely measured input via SDEdit-like denoising, with PDE-residual gradients in training and inference. L2 accuracy is similar to (marginally better than) a direct-mapping CNN, but the model is much more robust to changes in the low-fidelity input and gives better kinetic-energy spectra. This is relevant because GRACE input characteristics vary between processing centres and releases. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Shu2023_PhysicsInformedDiffusionFlow.pdf).

**huang2024diffusionpde**. Huang, J., Yang, G., Wang, Z., Park, J. J. (2024). DiffusionPDE: generative PDE-solving under partial observation. NeurIPS 2024 (venue from the PDF footer). arXiv:2406.17763. The model learns the joint distribution of coefficients and solutions. Denoising is guided by sparse measurements plus PDE-residual guidance, and it beats existing solvers under partial observation. It is currently limited to slices of static systems. This is a joint-state analogue, e.g., storage plus fluxes. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Huang2024_DiffusionPDE.pdf).

**lippe2023pderefiner**. Lippe, P., Veeling, B. S., Perdikaris, P., Turner, R. E., Brandstetter, J. (2023). PDE-Refiner: achieving accurate long rollouts with neural PDE solvers. NeurIPS 2023 (venue from the PDF footer). arXiv:2308.05732. A large-scale analysis shows that MSE-trained neural solvers neglect low-amplitude, often high-frequency, components, and this limits rollout accuracy. Diffusion-like iterative refinement models all frequency components and stays accurate longer, at higher compute per step. This is the spectral argument for diffusion over MSE regression. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Lippe2023_PDERefiner.pdf).

### D. Probabilistic forecasting, ensembles and nowcasting

**price2025gencast**. Price, I., Sanchez-Gonzalez, A., Alet, F., Andersson, T. R., El-Kadi, A., Masters, D., et al. (2025; online Dec 2024). Probabilistic weather forecasting with machine learning. *Nature* 637, 84. DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-08252-9; arXiv:2312.15796 (GenCast).
A conditional diffusion model with a graph-transformer on a sphere mesh produces 15-day 0.25° global ensembles in 12 h steps in 8 minutes. It beats ECMWF ENS on 97.4% of 1320 targets on CRPS, and is evaluated with spread–skill and rank histograms against 50-member ENS. This is the reference for diffusion ensembles and evaluation protocol. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Price2025_GenCast.pdf, arXiv version; abstract, baselines and evaluation sections checked).

**li2024seeds**. Li, L., Carver, R., Lopez-Gomez, I., Sha, F., Anderson, J. (2024). Generative emulation of weather forecast ensembles with diffusion models. *Science Advances* 10, eadk4489. DOI 10.1126/sciadv.adk4489; arXiv:2306.14066 (SEEDS). A diffusion model conditioned on K = 1–2 "seeding" GEFS members generates hundreds to thousands of members. Two modes are trained: emulation, which matches the 31-member GEFS, and post-processing, which targets a GEFS/ERA5 mixture to correct biases. The post-processing mode gives more reliable ensembles and better extreme-event probabilities. This is a template for amplifying a few GRACE solution variants (centres, filters) into a calibrated ensemble. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Li2024_SEEDS.pdf).

**zhong2025fuxiens**. Zhong, X., Chen, L., Li, H., Liu, J., Fan, X., Feng, J., et al. (2025). FuXi-ENS: a machine learning model for efficient and accurate ensemble weather prediction. *Science Advances*. DOI 10.1126/sciadv.adu2854; arXiv:2405.05925. A 0.25°, 6-hourly, 15-day ensemble system. It is **VAE-based, not diffusion**: members come from learned Gaussian perturbations, with a CRPS loss plus a KL term aligning the model-derived and target distributions. Unlike GenCast and SEEDS, it needs no EDA or NWP members. This is a non-diffusion probabilistic baseline. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Zhong2024_FuXiENS.pdf).

**couairon2024arches**. Couairon, G., Singh, R., Charantonis, A., Lessig, C., Monteleoni, C. (2024). ArchesWeather & ArchesWeatherGen: a deterministic and generative model for efficient ML weather forecasting. arXiv:2412.12971. A deterministic 1.5° model (ArchesWeather) plus a flow-matching model (ArchesWeatherGen) that maps its predictions to the full distribution of ERA5 states, i.e., residual generative modelling. It surpasses IFS ENS and NeuralGCM on most variables in CRPS, ensemble-mean RMSE and Brier score. **Key lesson:** the main obstacle to residual modelling is *overfitting of the deterministic model*. Training the residual on data the deterministic model has not seen, and scaling the initial sampling noise, fix it. This applies directly to a CorrDiff-style GRACE design with few months of data. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Couairon2024_ArchesWeatherGen.pdf).

**ravuri2021dgmr**. Ravuri, S., Lenc, K., Willson, M., Kangin, D., Lam, R., Mirowski, P., et al. (2021). Skilful precipitation nowcasting using deep generative models of radar. *Nature* 597, 672. DOI 10.1038/s41586-021-03854-z; arXiv:2104.00954 (DGMR). A conditional GAN nowcaster for 5–90 min lead times over regions up to 1536 × 1280 km. Over 50 Met Office forecasters ranked it first for accuracy and usefulness in **88%** of cases against two competitive methods. This is the landmark case of generative models fixing blurry deterministic predictions. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Ravuri2021_DGMR.pdf, arXiv version) · **LOW_RELEVANCE**.

**leinonen2023ldcast**. Leinonen, J., Hamann, U., Nerini, D., Germann, U., Franch, G. (2023). Latent diffusion models for generative precipitation nowcasting with accurate uncertainty quantification. arXiv:2304.12891. The latent diffusion nowcaster (4 input frames, 20 output frames of 5 min) beats DGMR and PySTEPS in CRPS. Its rank distributions are much closer to uniform, so its UQ is better. It is more stable and cheaper to train than GANs but more expensive to sample. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Leinonen2023_LDCast.pdf) · **LOW_RELEVANCE**.

**gao2023prediff**. Gao, Z., Shi, X., Han, B., Wang, H., Jin, X., Maddix, D., et al. (2023). PreDiff: precipitation nowcasting with latent diffusion models. NeurIPS 2023 (venue confirmed from the PDF footer). arXiv:2307.10422. A conditional latent diffusion nowcaster with "knowledge alignment". At each denoising step it estimates the deviation from an imposed constraint and adjusts the transition distribution. The constraints are energy conservation (N-body MNIST) and anticipated precipitation intensity (SEVIR). This is a *soft*, guidance-style constraint, not an exact one. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Gao2023_PreDiff.pdf; abstract and method passage).

**gong2024cascast**. Gong, J., Bai, L., Ye, P., Xu, W., Liu, N., Dai, J., et al. (2024). CasCast: skillful high-resolution precipitation nowcasting via cascaded modelling. arXiv:2402.04290. A deterministic model predicts mesoscale distributions. A latent diffusion transformer (CasFormer, frame-wise guided) generates small-scale patterns, which improves extremes. This is the cascade/residual design in nowcasting. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Gong2024_CasCast.pdf) · **LOW_RELEVANCE**.

**finn2024seaice**. Finn, T. S., Durand, C., Farchi, A., Bocquet, M., Brajard, J. (2024). Towards diffusion models for large-scale sea-ice modelling. arXiv:2406.18417. The paper compares *unconditional* generation of Arctic-wide multivariate neXtSIM sea-ice states by latent diffusion against data-space diffusion. **LDMs produce smoother fields and lose small-scale information** because of the VAE latent mapping, although their scores are similar. Both models shift toward higher energies than the test set (train 2000–2014, test 2016–2018). For GRACE, prefer data-space diffusion or a high-channel latent space if fine scales matter. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Finn2024_SeaIceDiffusion.pdf).

### E. Data assimilation and reconstruction from sparse observations

**rozet2023sda**. Rozet, F., Louppe, G. (2023). Score-based data assimilation. arXiv:2306.10574. NeurIPS 2023 (venue confirmed from the PDF footer). Trajectory-level score models are trained on short segments and composed with pseudo-Markov blankets. Observations enter only at inference through a new Gaussian likelihood-score approximation, so retraining is not needed for new observation operators. This is the methodological base for GRACE+wells or GRACE+GNSS fusion. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Rozet2023_SDA.pdf).

**rozet2023sdaqg**. Rozet, F., Louppe, G. (2023). Score-based data assimilation for a two-layer quasi-geostrophic model. arXiv:2310.01853. This short workshop paper scales SDA to a 2-layer QG model (256×256). Gradient checkpointing and inverted-bottleneck blocks cut memory by 72% for 27% more time. The local score network is trained on 9-step segments from 1024 trajectories. Assimilating sparse station velocities over 32 days recovers the trajectories, but samples show *less small-scale detail* than the simulations. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Rozet2023_SDA_QG.pdf).

**huang2024diffda**. Huang, L., Gianinazzi, L., Yu, Y., Dueben, P. D., Hoefler, T. (2024). DiffDA: a diffusion model for weather-scale data assimilation. arXiv:2401.05932. GraphCast is repurposed as a denoiser, and sparse observations are conditioned only at inference (inpainting/repaint). This produces 0.25° analyses usable to initialise forecasts. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Huang2024_DiffDA.pdf).

**manshausen2025genda**. Manshausen, P., Cohen, Y., Harrington, P., Pathak, J., Pritchard, M., Garg, P., et al. (2025). Generative data assimilation of sparse weather station observations at kilometer scales. *JAMES*. DOI 10.1029/2024MS004505; arXiv:2406.16947.
An unconditional EDM on HRRR analyses plus SDA of a few dozen stations beats HRRR analyses on held-out station winds, with similar precipitation error. The model reconstructs unobserved variables through learned multivariate relations, and reports a spread–skill and rank-histogram evaluation. This is direct evidence that point observations can steer a learned km-scale prior, as wells could with GRACE. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Manshausen2024_GenerativeDA.pdf).

**andry2025appa**. Andry, G., Lewin, S., Rozet, F., Rochman, O., Mangeleer, V., Pirlet, M., et al. (2025). Appa: bending weather dynamics with latent diffusion models for global data assimilation. arXiv:2504.18720. A 565M-parameter latent diffusion model on ERA5 (0.25°, 1 h) handles reanalysis, filtering and forecasting in one model, conditioned on arbitrary observations through moment-matching posterior sampling (MMPS, Jacobian-linearised for non-linear operators). Performance is competitive across tasks without retraining. The authors list limitations: synthetic observations only, latent compression "inevitably degrades fine-scale information", posterior calibration not yet validated, and costly decoding during conditioning. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Andry2025_Appa.pdf).

**qu2024deepgenda**. Qu, Y., Nathaniel, J., Li, S., Gentine, P. (2024). Deep generative data assimilation in multimodal setting. CVPRW 2024, DOI 10.1109/CVPRW63382.2024.00050; arXiv:2404.06665. SLAMS is score-based latent DA. An encoder takes the background state concatenated with in situ and ex situ (satellite) modalities as channels, and denoising happens in a shared latent space. Robustness is tested with synthetically coarsened, noised and sparsified observations. Latent multimodal assimilation is more robust than pixel-space assimilation. This is a template for fusing GRACE with heterogeneous products. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Qu2024_DeepGenDA.pdf).

**martin2025genda**. Martin, S. A., Manucharyan, G. E., Klein, P. (2025). Generative data assimilation for surface ocean state estimation from multi-modal satellite observations. *JAMES* 17. DOI 10.1029/2025MS005063 (preprint EarthArXiv 10.31223/X5ZT6N). A diffusion prior on GLORYS (1/12°) is guided by sparse satellite SSH and SST to infer currents and salinity without retraining (GenDA). **PAYWALLED** (OA, blocked: Wiley and EarthArXiv returned HTML to scripted download). Summary based on the abstract and the code README.

**asefi2025lagrangian**. Asefi, N., Lupin-Jimenez, L., Wu, T., He, R., Chattopadhyay, A. (2025). Generative Lagrangian data assimilation for ocean dynamics under extreme sparsity. arXiv:2507.06479. A DDPM conditioned on Fourier-neural-operator predictions reconstructs surface ocean fields from Lagrangian observations at 99% sparsity (synthetic) and 99.9% (real satellite). It improves fine-scale structure, spectra, vorticity and strain over DL baselines, and needs no forward dynamical model (ROMS/MOM6). The design is "deterministic operator first, then conditional diffusion". **FULLTEXT_READ** (Asefi2025_LagrangianGenDA.pdf).

**li2026sage**. Li, H., Wang, Y., Yang, K., Huang, G., Xia, X., Chen, Z., et al. (2026). Probabilistic reconstruction of global sea surface temperature using generative diffusion models. arXiv:2603.16272. SAGE learns a prior from historical SST. At each step it applies an observation-consistency term with a mask and tunable guidance. Progressive fusion uses FY-3D satellites for basin-scale structure and in situ data for local anomalies and extremes. It beats operational products against independent in situ data and improves 10-day forecasts. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Li2026_SSTDiffusionRecon.pdf; abstract and method passages).

**qian2026gencrest**. Qian, Z., Liu, T., Chan, D., Bathiany, S., Yang, S., Hess, P., et al. (2026). Generative deep learning improves reconstruction of global historical climate records. arXiv:2602.16515. GenCRest is pretrained on monthly-mean fields from 20 historical simulations of 10 CMIP6 models (1850–2014), then trained on ERA5. It reconstructs complete *monthly* temperature and precipitation from sparse, uncertain observations by likelihood guidance that accounts for observational error. Evaluation uses HadCRUT5 masks with perturbations from the observational-error covariance, and the model yields ensembles of plausible histories. This is the closest analogue to reconstructing pre-2002 TWS or bridging the GRACE gap with error covariances. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Qian2026_GenHistoricalClimate.pdf; abstract, method and training passages).

**song2025reconmost**. Song, Y., Lyu, P., Fei, B., Ling, F., Ouyang, W., Bai, L. (2025). ReconMOST: multi-layer sea temperature reconstruction with observations-guided diffusion. arXiv:2506.10391. An unconditional diffusion prior is pretrained on CMIP6. Sparse in situ (buoy-like) points act as gradient-guidance points in the reverse process, using a soft-extension kernel. Up to 92.5% of the data can be missing, and the method is tested on CMIP6 and EN4. The authors note that the dependence on guidance strength is underexplored and that the method is static, with no temporal dynamics yet. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Song2025_ReconMOST.pdf).

### F. Gap filling and inpainting of satellite and geophysical products

**barth2024dindiff**. Barth, A., Brajard, J., Alvera-Azcárate, A., Mohamed, B., Troupin, C., Beckers, J.-M. (2024). **zou2023diffcr**. Zou, X., Li, K., Xing, J., Zhang, Y., Wang, S., Jin, L., Tao, P. (2023). Optical RGB cloud removal only. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Zou2023_DiffCR.pdf; abstract and method) · **LOW_RELEVANCE**.

**chen2026mbesddpm**. Chen, J., Wu, Z., Zhao, D., Bu, X., Zhou, J., Shang, J., Wang, M., Liu, Y. (2026). MBES-DDPM: multibeam echo sounder bathymetry swath gap reconstruction based on denoising diffusion probability model. *Remote Sensing* 18(3), 496. DOI 10.3390/rs18030496.
A DDPM fills multibeam swath gaps. **SIO V32.1 altimetry-derived free-air gravity anomalies** are injected as a macro-topographic prior. RMSE falls by at least 34% against baselines. This is a geodetic gravity-conditioned diffusion example. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Chen2026_MBESDDPM.pdf).

**hou2025gravmag**. Hou, Z., Shen, J., Zhao, X., Wei, J., Ding, H., Zhao, X., Wang, J. (2025). Gravity and magnetic field reconstruction using a diffusion model. *Mathematical Geosciences* 58, 1909–1940 (issue dated 2026). DOI 10.1007/s11004-025-10255-9.

### G. Hydrology-specific diffusion

**ou2025drum**. Ou, Z., Nai, C., Pan, B., Zheng, Y., Shen, C., Jiang, P., et al. (2025). Probabilistic diffusion models advance extreme flood forecasting. *GRL* 52, e2025GL115705. A diffusion model with an LSTM backbone on CAMELS basins improves top-1‰ flow nowcasting in 72.3% of basins and extends lead times by about 1 day for 20- and 50-year floods. **yang2026hdiffusion**. Yang, W., Ji, H., Lonzarich, L., Song, Y., Shen, C., et al. (2026). Diffusion-based probabilistic modeling for hourly streamflow prediction and assimilation. *WRR* 62, e2025WR042720. DOI 10.1029/2025WR042720; arXiv:2510.08488.
h-Diffusion is conditioned on daily observed or simulated discharge to generate hourly hydrographs, a form of *temporal downscaling*. h-Diffusion-DA assimilates recent observations by inpainting without retraining. Median NSE is 0.774, rising to 0.827 with inpainting and 0.844 with multiple forcings, across 516 CAMELS basins, with positive CRPSS for high flows. I found no conservation of daily volume in the text. This is an analogue for GRACE monthly-to-submonthly downscaling. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Yang2026_hDiffusion.pdf, arXiv version).

**wang2025hydrodiffusion**. Wang, Y., Yu, A., Zhang, L., Varadharajan, C., Erichson, N. B. (2025). HydroDiffusion: diffusion-based probabilistic streamflow forecasting with a state space backbone. arXiv:2512.12183. It differs from DRUM in three ways: a continuous-time score-based formulation, joint denoising of the whole forecast trajectory instead of autoregression, and a decoder-only SSM backbone instead of LSTMs. The result is temporally coherent ensembles that beat DRUM on CAMELS (531 basins). The authors stress the need for strong hydrological baselines. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Wang2025_HydroDiffusion.pdf).

**bhavsar2026hydrots**. Bhavsar, F., Benoit, L., Savatier, M., Gabriel, E. (2026). Transformer-based diffusion models for hydrological time series probabilistic imputation and forecasting. arXiv:2607.21200. A modified CSDI (convolutions, multi-scale layers, RMSNorm) jointly imputes and forecasts sub-daily water quantity and quality at 6 sites over more than 15 years in NE France. It captures uncertainty better than baselines and is competitive on point metrics. Inter-variable correlation is modelled imperfectly. This is a template for imputing gaps in TWS time series, such as the 2017–2018 gap. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Bhavsar2026_HydroTSDiffusion.pdf).

**jiao2026sparsewell**. Jiao, Z., Xiong, G., Han, C., Xu, H., Meng, Y., Wu, J., Yang, Y., Wang, J., Wu, J., Zhu, X. (2026). Sparse-well diffusion reconstructions enable groundwater transport equation recovery and plume refinement. *GRL* 53. DOI 10.1029/2026GL126144. Diffusion reconstruction of groundwater plumes from sparse wells. This is the only groundwater diffusion paper found. **PAYWALLED** (OA, blocked: Wiley pdfdirect refused scripted download). Summary based on the title and metadata only.

### H. Climate emulators and generative foundation models

**bassetti2024diffesm**. Bassetti, S., Hutchinson, B., Tebaldi, C., Kravitz, B. (2024). DiffESM: conditional emulation of temperature and precipitation in Earth system models with 3D diffusion models. *JAMES*. DOI 10.1029/2023MS004194; arXiv:2304.11699. A 3D (space-time) diffusion model is conditioned on monthly-mean maps (96×96 global grid) and produces daily temperature or precipitation "consistent with those averages". It uses classifier-free guidance on day labels. Consistency with the monthly mean is learned, not enforced. This is a direct template for GRACE monthly-to-daily disaggregation. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Bassetti2023_DiffESM.pdf, arXiv version; method passages).

**wattmeyer2023ace**. Watt-Meyer, O., Dresdner, G., McGibbon, J., Clark, S. K., Henn, B., Duncan, J., et al. (2023). ACE: a fast, skillful learned global atmospheric model for climate prediction. arXiv:2310.02074. A deterministic, 200M-parameter, autoregressive SFNO emulator of 100 km FV3GFS. It is stable for 100 years and beats a baseline on more than 90% of tracked variables, about 100× faster. It **"nearly conserves column moisture without explicit constraints"**. The formulation allows checking mass and moisture budgets, but there is a global surface-pressure bias of about −25 Pa, whereas the reference conserves dry air mass exactly. Diagnosing conservation, not enforcing it, is the GRACE-relevant point. **FULLTEXT_READ** (WattMeyer2023_ACE.pdf).

**ruhlingcachay2024sphericaldyffusion**. Rühling Cachay, S., Henn, B., Watt-Meyer, O., Bretherton, C. S., Yu, R. (2024). Probabilistic emulation of a global climate model with Spherical DYffusion. NeurIPS 2024 (venue from the PDF footer). arXiv:2406.14798. The dynamics-informed diffusion of DYffusion (time as the "noise" axis) is combined with an SFNO backbone. It gives stable 100-year, 6-hourly ensemble climate emulation of coarse FV3GFS with low overhead relative to deterministic baselines, and near gold-standard climate statistics. **FULLTEXT_READ** (RuhlingCachay2024_SphericalDYffusion.pdf).

**brenowitz2025cbottle**. Brenowitz, N. D., Ge, T., Subramaniam, A., Manshausen, P., Gupta, A., Hall, D. M., et al. (2025). Climate in a Bottle: towards a generative foundation model for the kilometer-scale global atmosphere. arXiv:2505.06474. cBottle is a two-stage diffusion model. A coarse global generator is conditioned only on monthly SST and time of year/day, followed by patch-based super-resolution to 5 km (about 12.5 M pixels per sample) with no autoregressive rollout. It also reconstructs missing or corrupted channels and is pitched for "data recovery" when records are incomplete. This is a template for a global TWS generator conditioned on climate drivers. **FULLTEXT_READ** (Brenowitz2025_cBottle.pdf; abstract and conclusions).

### I. Remote-sensing super-resolution and reviews

**liu2024diffrs**. Liu, Y., Yue, J., Xia, S., Ghamisi, P., Xie, W., Fang, L. (2024). Diffusion models meet remote sensing: principles, methods, and perspectives. *IEEE TGRS* 62, 4708322. DOI 10.1109/TGRS.2024.3464685; arXiv:2404.08926. **hadid2024geogenai**. Hadid, A., Chakraborty, T., Busby, D. (2024). ## Checks of the preliminary review (preliminary_review_2026-11-12.md, Sections 4.3, 5 and 6)

Each cited adjacent-geoscience reference was verified (DOI or arXiv ID above). Claims were checked against the full text where available.

**Confirmed as stated:** CorrDiff (25 km to 2 km, Taiwan); R2-D2 (45 km to 9 km, CRPS more than 40% lower than BCSD); Tomasi (LDM beats a GAN with the same U-Net backbone); Ribeiro (5 years of training with a frozen Prithvi-WxC encoder); Bischoff & Deck (noise to an intermediate time and denoise with the high-res model, though the paper chains *two* models); Hess (noise level at the PSD intersection); Tie (area-averaging operator; supervised models fall below the coarse input on real GCM data); Chakraborty (multiple observation types); Suresh Babu (conditional beats guided for fine scales); Harder (hard constraints help, soft penalties leave violations and worsen RMSE, hard constraints fail with inconsistent sources); Singh (small gains relative to cost; across-seed variability exceeds within-seed); CorrDiff and R2-D2 under-dispersive; Rampal 2026 (clear lead for precipitation, marginal for temperature); Rampal 2025 and Najafi (realism does not guarantee change signals; Najafi checked at abstract level only); Hou (interpolation and denoising of exploration gravity/magnetic grids); Chen (gravity used as a bathymetry prior; the gravity is SIO V32.1, which is altimetry-derived, so "altimetric gravity" is correct).

**Claims to correct or qualify:**
1. **Ling et al. 2024 (§5.1)** is presented as narrowing the gap to GRACE's small samples by downscaling "monthly fields". The model was **trained on 6-hourly data, 1961–2015**, and applied to monthly means under an assumed scale-invariance. It is not evidence that diffusion trains well on a monthly-sized sample. The same caveat applies to Ribeiro and Addison: their 5 or 14 *years* of training data are *daily* samples, which is roughly 1,800–5,000 fields against about 250 for GRACE.
2. **Forestier et al. 2026 (§5.2)**: "The diffusion model only slightly outperformed a CNN" is not what the paper says. It reports accuracy "comparable to a strong convolutional baseline under RMSE/SSIM" and says the approach "should not be interpreted as providing a large RMSE improvement over RESAC". "Uncalibrated" is a fair paraphrase only in the sense that calibration was *not tested*. The paper says a calibrated product "would require testing the relationship between ensemble spread and actual reconstruction error".
3. **Han et al. 2024 (§5.2)** is listed under "generative priors with guidance ... removes the need for paired data". KESHDiff is a **conditionally trained** diffusion model: the low-resolution SSH is an input to the U-Net in the reverse process, trained on reanalysis. It belongs with the supervised conditional designs. Its relevance is sim-to-observation transfer validated on independent along-track data, which the review states correctly.
4. **§5.3, "No implementation was found in any field"** of a conservation constraint generalised beyond block means: this is overstated for generative models. Bochow et al. (2026, *The Cryosphere*) apply a Harder-style hard conservation constraint *during consistency-model inference* without retraining. Tie et al. (2026) put an area-weighted conservative averaging operator inside DPS guidance. Projected diffusion (Christopher et al. 2024) and ECI sampling (Cheng et al. 2024) enforce general linear or convex constraints exactly. No implementation exists for a GRACE-type operator (SH truncation, filtering or mascon kernel with correlated errors). Because that operator is linear, existing projection and guidance methods apply directly. The statement should be narrowed accordingly.
5. **Hess et al. 2025 (§5.2)**: the review is correct, but it should note that the paper reports two inconsistent values of t* (0.468 in Methods, 0.63 in the SI). The paper also states that the global mean is "only approximately conserved".
6. **Bischoff & Deck (§5.2), "The simplest version adds noise ... and denoises with the prior"**: this is essentially right, but the published method uses *two* independently trained diffusion models (source and target domains) chained into a bridge, not a single prior.
7. **Lopez-Gomez et al. reference**: the PNAS title is "Dynamical-generative downscaling of climate model ensembles". "R2-D2" is the model name used inside the paper, which is fine.
8. Minor: the reference list gives Harder et al. (2023) in JMLR without volume. It is **JMLR 24(365):1–40**. Suresh Babu et al. is now published in **JAMES 18, e2025MS005324** (DOI 10.1029/2025MS005324). Chakraborty et al. has DOI 10.1088/3049-4753/ae286e. Martin et al. GenDA is published in **JAMES (2025), DOI 10.1029/2025MS005063**.
